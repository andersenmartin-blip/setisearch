# Rubin photometry and detector review before the main sample

**The API schema supports a direct science-image flux analysis, but neither a signed difference-flux curve nor an alert count establishes an evaluable stellar-dimming sample.** No main-sample flux was read for this review. The proposed detector below is a development-stage prescription; freeze its final parameters, selection, quality policy and code after the separate development audit and before opening main-sample fluxes. The original approved plan remains unchanged. The root session records the user's later authorization to accelerate Rubin work.

## Source-grounded constraints

Official Fink documentation distinguishes difference `psfFlux`, direct `scienceFlux`, and `templateFlux`, with measurements in nJy. Its warning is material: independently measured difference flux is not exactly measured science flux minus measured template flux. DiaSource-based and DiaObject-based forced fluxes also use different centroids. Use a consistent direct-science stream; never reconstruct a supposedly exact total flux by adding difference and template measurements. [Photometry](https://doc.lsst.fink-broker.org/data/photometry/).

The local API schemas obtained by the access pilot expose direct `scienceFlux` and `scienceFluxErr` in both `/sources` and `/fp`. The forced table has `diaForcedSourceId`, `visit`, `detector`, `band` and processing/withdrawal times, but **no measurement-quality flags**. The source table exposes the science-fit flags `forced_PsfFlux_flag`, `_edge`, and `_noGoodPixels`, alongside centroid/pixel flags. Field presence does not establish that a returned value is populated. The official APDB definitions were updated 30 October 2026. [APDB schema](https://sdm-schemas.lsst.io/apdb.html) · [API schema access](https://doc.lsst.fink-broker.org/services/api/definitions/).

Fink warns about unreliable early reliability scores, missing fields, split object associations and duplicated historical records. Its issues page describes a sub-six-minute association limitation; do not merge nearby records merely because their timestamps or positions are close. Science/template centroid descriptions differ slightly between the general Fink guide and current field schema; preserve the exact product definition and avoid relying on cross-stream algebra. [Quality](https://doc.lsst.fink-broker.org/data/quality/) · [Known issues](https://doc.lsst.fink-broker.org/data/issues/).

Rubin's public alert product is triggered by positive or negative difference detections and carries up to twelve months of associated history. This is a maximum historical window, not proof of complete visit coverage. Upstream masks, source association and reliability filtering already remove some events; current documentation describes rejecting reliability below 0.5 and some negative direct-image measurements. Missing alerts, unavailable forced rows and empty responses are not constant-flux measurements. [Alert product](https://prompt-products.lsst.io/products/alerts/index.html) · [Upstream filtering](https://prompt-products.lsst.io/processing/filtering/index.html).

## Flux quantities and the 0.3-magnitude condition

For positive direct total flux F in nJy, m_AB = 31.4 - 2.5 log10(F). At sufficiently high signal-to-noise, sigma_m approximately equals (2.5 / ln 10) sigma_F / F; do not apply this symmetric approximation at low signal-to-noise. A positive same-band baseline B defines a dimming estimate delta_m = -2.5 log10(F/B). Therefore delta_m >= 0.3 corresponds exactly to F/B <= 10^(-0.12) = **0.7585775750291838**, or a fractional loss of at least **0.2414224249708162**. These are algebraic consequences of the magnitude definition, not an empirical sensitivity result.

Preserve signed fluxes and positive uncertainty estimates. Never take log(abs(F)), assign a magnitude to F<=0, or replace missing photometry with zero. A nonpositive direct-flux estimate may still pass a linear-flux deficit test, but it receives no finite point-estimate magnitude and requires explicit fit/image-quality review. Its status cannot be promoted by a missing failure flag. Negative difference flux alone supplies neither B nor the fractional stellar dimming.

Direct `/fp` science flux is preferred for uniform DiaObject centroids when its available quality control is adequate. Direct `/sources` science flux can be analyzed as a separate, detection-conditioned stream with its own baseline. **Do not concatenate these streams as extra visits or switch between them in response to a particular dip.** A single template value is context, not an automatically accepted substitute for a stable multi-epoch baseline. Any alternative reconstruction requires a separate, validated development protocol with its covariance/systematic limitations retained.

## Proposed simple detector, to finalize using development only

This deliberately conservative proposal returns numerical anomalies; its score is not a Gaussian significance or global false-alarm probability.

1. Fix the photometric stream for each analysis tier, the primary UTC month, and a baseline context interval before main-sample flux reads. A bounded same-band context of at most 30 days on each side of the tested visit is the proposed default; any change requires development-only justification and a frozen configuration.
2. Preserve every unique measurement and every exclusion reason. A numerical row needs a valid band/time, finite direct science flux and finite strictly positive scienceFluxErr. Reject withdrawn versions and explicit critical failure flags. Missing required flags remain `QUALITY_UNKNOWN`, never False. Retain numerically useful forced rows lacking flags in a separate quality-limited tier; do not count them as fully quality-vetted observations. Join a source's flags to a forced row only on an unambiguous object/visit/detector/band match, retain the centroid difference and never assume this certifies a different fit.
3. For each tested row, form the baseline from otherwise admitted same-band rows in the fixed context, excluding **every row on that tested UTC date**. Require at least five distinct baseline visits across at least three other UTC dates. Do not clip points because they are faint, and do not discard potential dips to manufacture the baseline. Keep the baseline visit IDs for every tested row.
4. Set B to the median baseline science flux. Let S=max(1.4826 median(|F_j-B|), median(sigma_j)). Require finite B>0 and B/S>=10. S is a conservative empirical reference scale, not the standard error of B; it is not divided by sqrt(N). This avoids falsely gaining precision from many correlated visits. Failures are `NO_USABLE_BASELINE`, not negative detections.
5. Record every numerically testable row and flag a proposed hit when F_i<=qB with q=10^(-0.12), and R=(B-F_i)/sqrt(sigma_i^2+S^2)>=5. The depth cut refers to the point estimate; R>=5 measures an empirical deficit relative to baseline and does **not** establish a five-sigma lower bound of 0.3 mag. Record F_i, sigma_i, B, S, ratio, R, actual baseline coverage and quality tier even when no hit occurs. For F_i<=0 store delta_m=null and `NONPOSITIVE_SCIENCE_ESTIMATE` alongside the flux criterion.
6. Preserve singletons and all threshold crossings. A connected event may group same-object/same-band hits under a fixed temporal rule, but the full per-visit hit table remains authoritative. Image review, Gaia association, blending/motion and variability context determine later dispositions. Do not veto a genuine dip merely because the difference sign is positive relative to a different template state.

The required baseline sample count, interval, SNR floor and R threshold are proposals, not claimed optimizations. If development shows they are unworkable, freeze one documented revision before main data. Do not loosen them after seeing a main-sample candidate or to reach fifty objects. No period search, trend fitting, iterative clipping or additional trial grid is hidden in this proposal.

## Time, identity and duplicate rules

`midpointMjdTai` is MJD in TAI at the effective visit midpoint; it is not JD or UTC. Keep the original double and derive UTC using `astropy.time.Time(value, format='mjd', scale='tai').utc`. Use TAI for elapsed SI-time calculations. Convert the approved UTC boundaries to TAI before comparison. With Astropy 8.0.2 and the installed leap-second table, the October interval is **[61314.00042824074, 61345.00042824074) MJD(TAI)**. Its UTC endpoints are 2026-10-01T00:00:00 and 2026-11-01T00:00:00. Do not label observation time as barycentric. Record the conversion/library version; no leap-second constant is hardcoded into the detector. [Astropy time](https://docs.astropy.org/en/stable/time/index.html).

Store all 64-bit identifiers as decimal strings or exact integers, never floating-point identifiers. Deduplicate source measurements by diaSourceId and forced measurements by diaForcedSourceId. Use object/visit/detector/band as a consistency key; flag conflicting repeated IDs or multiple physical versions. When processing/withdrawal metadata permits, select the latest non-withdrawn processing version using a frozen rule and retain all alternatives. If that ordering is ambiguous, preserve a conflict and omit it from quantitative scoring. Never select a duplicate because its flux is more extreme or closer to baseline. Source and forced estimates of one visit are alternative measurements, not independent observing time. Packet histories repeatedly include prior measurements; count unique visits, not packet count. Nearby split diaObjectIds require a separate positional/proper-motion/association review before merging.

## Recovery control: 36, not 108

The approved arithmetic is **3 depths x 3 durations x 4 placements = 36 injections**. Three depths such as 0.3, 0.6 and 1.0 mag are a proposed development choice; freeze the three actual durations from development cadence/exposure metadata. Four new placements per depth-duration combination should include two observation-centered and two gap-centered cases under a deterministic metadata-only placement rule. Repeated positions and shared source curves must be reported; they do not create independent trials.

For a top-hat decrement of depth d, inject F'_i=F_i-alpha_i B(1-10^(-0.4d)), where alpha_i is the exposure fraction inside the injected interval when exposure duration is available. This preserves the existing measured residuals and quoted errors rather than spuriously scaling noise with the signal. If exposure durations are unavailable, use a declared midpoint-sampling approximation and do not claim sub-exposure recovery. Keep the original flux/error/flags and run the complete frozen detector, including recomputed baseline eligibility, on each injected curve. No retuning follows these controls.

Count all 36 cases. Report separately no overlapping visits, sampled-but-baseline-ineligible, sampled-but-missed, and recovered; report unconditional and sampling-conditional recovery by depth and duration. Gap events with zero sampled visits are misses in the unconditional denominator. Run unchanged control curves with the same method. Fixed quoted errors make this a digital after-processing test, not a calibrated photon-noise experiment or a measurement of upstream alert completeness.

## Feasibility decision and minimum pilot

**Schema-level decision: conditionally feasible; fifty evaluable objects not yet established.** The access pilot has identified the necessary direct-flux/error/time/band/identity fields, but has not yet supplied fifty development-independent light curves with a usable baseline. Its metadata reports October coverage on two nights; this must be treated as returned archive coverage and verified against the saved query, not assumed survey-wide observing history. Earlier same-band history may supply context only under the frozen context rule.

Report at least: metadata-selected objects, returned objects, unique source rows, unique forced rows, distinct visits per band, finite-flux/error rows, quality-known rows, quality-unknown rows, baseline-evaluable tested visits, and objects with at least one evaluable primary-window visit. Count main-sample objects only after those checks. Development objects are excluded from the main minimum. If fewer than fifty survive, deliver a forstudie/access study under the original plan; if none have required data, mark the scientific search blocked. An empty hit table from an unevaluable sample is not a null result.

The minimum fifty-object pilot also requires the complete event list, all thirty-six controls with sampling losses, disposition/image-control status, and reproduction. Availability of a catalog match or an API endpoint alone does not meet it. The proposed strict numerical tier and the quality-limited tier must retain separate denominators.
