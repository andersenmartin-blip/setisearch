# A separate May reference-quality follow-up

This experiment is motivated after the completed two-spectrum BERV comparison. Its question is whether a metadata-selected, higher-average-SNR May reference provides useful local profiles at a large barycentric velocity displacement. It is explicitly a posthoc follow-up with its own prospective acquisition and comparison protocol. The completed 24-profile experiment remains intact, including its low-quality May reference and all weak results.

The exact candidate is `ADP.2017-05-21T01:01:50.278`, raw progenitor `HARPS.2017-05-20T02:21:45.847`, on HD122640. Among the nine unused May 2017 products in the saved 18-product pool, it has the largest finite positive average metadata SNR (77.8). Ties would use the lexicographically smallest product ID. The used May reference has average SNR 16.6. These metadata values do not establish local blue-window quality or calibrated sensitivity. No new spectrum or line-window value enters the selection.

One primary header is permitted: one attempt, 30 seconds, 512 KiB, no redirects or retries. Exact DP/raw identity, target, time, position, exposure duration, BARYCENT frame and high-resolution fiber state must pass the inherited checks. The recorded BERV must differ in absolute value from each of the six HD122640 discovery-record BERVs by at least ten nominal resolution elements, `10 × 299792.458 / 115000 = 26.06890939130435 km/s`. This fixed descriptive benchmark selects a substantially displaced comparison; it is neither a classification rule nor a statistical-power statement. The candidate need not increase the already known 40.363479 km/s span.

Only a passing header can release one exact spectrum download: one attempt, 60 seconds, 16 MiB, no redirects or retries. Product bytes and identity are preserved. Full FITS table representation must pass before spectral-array access: one row, air wavelengths in Angstrom, ADU flux, inherited resolution and exact header/provenance consistency. Every failure or incompatibility is retained and ends this phase without selecting the next-best candidate.

The comparison retains all ten queue identities. All six HD122640 records are measured in both inherited reference frames, for at most twelve new profile slots: V2E2-0229, V2E2-0231, V2E2-0234, V2E2-0235, V2E2-0236 and V2E2-0237. The four HD135204 records remain explicitly without a selected new epoch. Fixed accepted centers, sampling, normalization, sidebands, zero-shift correlations and signed-width templates are inherited unchanged. There is no shift optimization, new detector, origin assignment, formal significance or label update.

The report must retain both earlier reference epochs and compare local continuum/noise context as well as profile correlations. It must show all six records, including the isolated 5548.09 Angstrom record. Correlated event records are not independent signals. Stronger barycentric coherence can be consistent with stellar spectral structure, while instrumental effects, wavelength calibration and stellar radial-velocity differences remain possible confounders. Weak correlations, even with higher average SNR, do not establish a transient or an artifact. No hypothesis is promoted by the outcome of this descriptive exercise.

The original 300-exposure full-spectrum survey and 669 V2 classifications remain unchanged. Rubin's completed 190-object feasibility study is not reopened by this protocol. Public GitHub updates are authorized by the user. The root freeze and exact public readback precede the new header; acquired bytes are preserved and read back before new array interpretation. All work uses existing public archival data, with no commissioned observations, paid services or unattended execution.
